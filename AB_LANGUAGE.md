# A/B — Language and the Referent

> **Historical name:** This page retains the original A/B terminology. The canonical reader-facing entry is [Language Referent](LANGUAGE_REFERENT.md). They name **the same referent**; finite operators called A and B keep their locally defined mathematical meaning.

> **A primitive relational language, deliberately open to movement.** Not a completed theory, a universal equation, or a replacement for another discipline.

## The referent is in motion

**The universe is in motion.** Earth changes; its materials, fields, and living systems interact. Human activity and the conditions in which it occurs also change.

Language moves, too, but **its established labels often lag behind what they describe.** A name may remain stable while the relationships it once identified have shifted. That gap creates a familiar error: **reification**, treating the description as though it were the thing itself.

A referent is not guaranteed to remain unchanged merely because a word does. Naming also becomes part of the encounter: it affects what can be communicated, compared, questioned, and followed. The task is **not to freeze the referent** but to retain enough **relational continuity** to recognize what has actually changed.

## Why A/B stays primitive

**Naming clarification — October 13, 2026:** *The language referent* is a reader-facing name for the **same referent** previously called A/B. It is not a second object, a replacement theory, or a boundary separating language from mathematics. James uses the clearer name so a reader doesn't confuse the continuing investigation with the specific finite mathematical generators labeled A and B. Naming supplies a reference that can be revisited and corrected without freezing what is observed.

Mathematics is itself human language; saying we "need mathematics to understand" the referent already puts language, mathematics, and understanding into relation. Discussing and correcting that sentence is itself another observation of the same referent, not a newly created second referent. Particular formal proofs still need their exact domains and witnesses; this conversational observation is not a universal theorem.

[James's language and observation report, with unedited quotations](https://github.com/huntingthereferent/huntingthereferent/blob/main/writing/LANGUAGE_AND_OBSERVATION.md) preserves the direct speech behind this correction.

A/B is language for approaching those moving relationships. Human beings cannot directly hand one another an unexpressed thought; investigation needs ways to express, examine, and revise what is being encountered.

**Its looseness serves a function.** A/B is not assigned a final mathematical object, fixed route, privileged scale, or particular machine. It remains free to **decompress** a compressed expression into its relevant relationships, **compress** a detailed relation into language that can travel, and shift perspective or representation as the subject demands.

These are **available movements, not a mandatory sequence.** The language need not finish one stage before another begins. Looking from another side does not automatically change the referent; neither does maintaining the same wording guarantee that the referent has been preserved.

**The referent is moving. The language must remain able to move with it.**

## The stepper

The stepper names **continuing relational movement**. What happens in a step may change **which step is possible next.** A resulting relation can be encountered again, from the same side or another, without requiring an unchanged object or a predetermined endpoint.

The recurring **two-step** is a useful continuation question: after one supported movement, does the resulting relationship still admit a further movement? This is not a universal binary law or a guarantee that every encountered relationship can advance.

The purpose is **continuity through change**, not motion at any price. If a relation cannot be followed, or evidence is missing, the uncertainty remains visible. Confidence may motivate exploration; it cannot certify an unwitnessed conclusion.

## Formalization and physical realization

Mathematics is itself a human formal language. It can make particular relationships precise, establish correspondences, exhibit counterexamples, and prove results within declared assumptions. Engineering may try to realize an identified relation in material systems. **Neither the equation nor the machine becomes the definition of A/B.**

The repository's finite-field constructions and other mathematical experiments therefore remain **scoped technical work**, not universal proofs of the language or evidence that a proposed physical mechanism exists.

James Muneoka's contribution is centered on observation and ordinary-language expression of relationships. Formal notation remains an area of active learning through continuing exposure and technical collaboration, including computational assistance. A result's **provenance and verification** remain distinct from personal notation fluency.

## What stays open

An individual mathematical problem can be solved. A mechanism can be constructed, measured, improved, or rejected. A description can be revised when the referent has changed. **None requires declaring the relational language itself complete.**

**Precision begins with the lens.** A/B starts by locating the referent in motion and keeping its relationships intelligible before fixing them in a particular description. Its primitive form lets that precision move with the referent—across changes in language, perspective, and representation—without confusing any one account of it with the thing being examined.
