# Separations — the main referent through the next movement

Related: [Human Drift](HUMAN_DRIFT.md) · [Regrounding](REGROUNDING.md) · [Pragmatics](PRAGMATICS.md)

## How this wording arrived

In a conversation about assistant misunderstandings and work moving between chat windows, the assistant described following the **“main referent through the next movement.”**

James answered:

> ah thats a better way to explain it ty "main referent through the next movement" that feels like one thing for james very good "seperation" :)

And then:

> i would like to put this in the repo for seperations thats useful for james or ppl who also can only feel through cross boundries

The phrase came from the assistant; James recognized something useful in it. Both parts of that exchange matter. The words are not presented as a finished definition of how James—or anyone else—thinks.

## What is being separated?

A separation can make two things easier to distinguish **without losing the connection between them**. For James, “main referent through the next movement” felt like one thing. The distinction did not cut it into a static referent on one side and a disconnected next step on the other. It made their continuity easier to express.

That matters when understanding happens across boundaries: from one chat window to another, from an example to a correction, from language to mathematics, from local execution to a new observation, or from what one person intended to what another person understood.

The boundaries are real differences. They do not automatically mean the relationship has ended or a new subject has begun.

## What happened in this conversation

James brought an excerpt from another chat window to show what had happened there. The assistant treated the excerpt as a fresh request to work on GitHub and began checking the repository. James corrected it: the excerpt was **reference material for this conversation**, not an instruction to resume that other task.

That correction was relevant to the same issue they had already been discussing: the assistant was losing the connection between the reason something was brought into the conversation and what it should do next.

The useful wording emerged when the assistant finally connected the example to the continuing discussion instead of opening another task. James recognized **“main referent through the next movement”** as a useful separation.

## Other places the distinction helps

- **Reference and instruction:** Something shown from another window may be context, not a new command. Understand why it was brought over before acting on it.
- **Correction and continuation:** Correcting a misunderstanding does not necessarily stop the work. The correction can change how the same relationship moves forward.
- **Assistant behavior and harness behavior:** A communication mistake belongs to the assistant's interpretation; an implementation mistake belongs to the implemented behavior. Both can matter to the same continuing work without being the same change.
- **Local result and crossed-boundary result:** A local PASS can support trying the next boundary. It is not the observation at that boundary. Carry the referent forward, attempt what is actually available, and learn from what happens.
- **Words and referent:** An example, name, analogy, or mathematical expression can change while the relationship being followed continues. Changing words neither guarantees sameness nor proves a new subject has appeared.

These are examples of one ordinary activity—making a distinction where it helps and keeping the relation that made the distinction useful. They are not five different procedures to install.

## For someone who recognizes this feeling

James described a way of feeling the connection **through crossing boundaries**. That expression is worth keeping in his own words.

Someone else may recognize the experience of a thought, relationship, or direction remaining continuous even as the conversation, medium, or activity changes. The wording gives people a way to compare and share how they experience that connection.

A useful separation should make it easier to say **what remains connected as something changes**. If the separation makes the original relationship harder to follow, it probably needs correcting too.
