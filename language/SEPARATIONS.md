# Separations — Preserve the Relation Across Boundaries

[Language overview](README.md) · [Regrounding](REGROUNDING.md)

A useful separation distinguishes contexts without pretending that a relationship automatically ends or survives between them. The continuing referent and an attempted next action should be identifiable separately, then related by observed evidence.

## Applied boundaries

- **Reference versus instruction:** a quoted or pasted conversation may be context rather than authorization for another task.
- **Interpretation versus execution:** the assistant's reading of an instruction is not evidence that a tool ran.
- **Local result versus new boundary:** a local PASS does not establish behavior after a handoff.
- **Words versus the thing described:** a new label is not proof of a new subject, and a retained label is not proof that nothing changed.

Retain actual source state, boundaries, and witnesses when continuing between contexts. Where evidence is missing, record UNKNOWN rather than invent continuity.

Retired conversational quotations are not reproduced in this technical account.
