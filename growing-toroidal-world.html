Straight. Boundary. Pivot. Continue.

A: travel straight. B: at the wall, change direction according to that wall's normal. A visible moving point leaves the resulting path in 2D, 3D, and 4D.

Exact boundary rule

Each trace moves straight within an n-dimensional box with coordinates between -1 and 1. For each axis, calculate the time until the next wall: tᵢ=(sign(vᵢ)-pᵢ)/vᵢ. Choose the nearest positive collision. At that wall, reverse only the velocity component perpendicular to the wall: vᵢ'=-vᵢ. Other velocity components are unchanged. Repeat. This is Pong-style reflection, without forces or curved motion.

2D uses a square, 3D a cube, and 4D a hypercube projected onto the screen. These are continuous box-billiard traces, not the earlier repository's finite-state transition graph. A finite display can stop after a chosen number of collisions; it does not establish mathematical completion of an infinite trajectory.
