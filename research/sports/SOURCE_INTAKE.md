# SPORTS — casual source scoping

Status: exploratory research, not a hosted service or autonomous data pipeline.
Environment: Tracework / autobots-assemble.

## Purpose
Use existing AI-assisted research to explore sports and sports-betting sources, compare what different observers report, and preserve interesting relationships, discrepancies and source locators. SPORTS is another subject within Tracework, not a new mathematical system or production data product.

## Approach
- Search broadly, including alternatives to the first source found. One provider is never the whole picture.
- Look for existing specialized sports-betting collectors, open data, odds comparison tools, archives, market movement records, league statistics and independent analysis.
- Read public information directly when available. Paid or restricted services may be noted as candidates without subscribing or integrating.
- Compare observations, including source independence, market timing, revisions, uncertainty and disagreement.
- Preserve concise source links, access dates, relevant excerpts or facts, and what remains unresolved.
- Continue through ordinary Tracework research handoffs when useful. Do not require a dedicated SPORTS AI agent or adapter to begin.
- Treat any betting-related inference as exploratory, not a verified edge or guaranteed prediction.

## Optional scoping note
For each useful source, record its name/link, what it covers, observed date, why it matters, alternatives and open questions. Distinguish an actually consulted source from a candidate link.

## Explicitly not required for casual scoping
No API keys, paid feed subscriptions, hosted service, cron schedule, permanent collector, credentials, provider registry, new orchestrator, or 100-run batch. Those are separate implementation decisions only if the research later warrants them.

## Evidence boundary
A source candidate is not a collected observation. A scoping note is not a runtime receipt. A comparison is not proof of predictive performance.
