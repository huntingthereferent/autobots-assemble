# WORLD Trial 26 — finite separation **between** observed source samples

The source-owned WORLD Trial25 verified three hypothetical segment–square intersection cases on a bounded 2,049-pose grid, while three other cases had no sampled intersection. The source reported 2,290 intersecting grid poses, and in all three detected cases a source vertex-to-face candidate fell within the earliest overlap-onset grid bracket. That evidence does **not** justify interpreting all intervening time as intersection-free.

## A distinct falsification question

Retain exactly the prior original WORLD21 conditional third impulse and its source-derived original finite face, finite receiver and 24×6 world. For all six admitted source cases, take the original Trial25 grid from `t=0.008` through `t=0.25` after that **unselected** impulse, with 2,048 time intervals per finite face. The two cases without any admitted third impulse remain absent.

For any given original sample pose, the maximum signed separation of the two finite objects along the square's *two local face normals at that instant* and the segment's normal is a conservative finite-object separating-axis margin. Each such axis is frozen in **world coordinates at that instant** when extended into neighboring times.

Every moving square corner has a uniform speed bound

`L = |v_center| + |angular_velocity| * sqrt(2) * square_half_extent`.

For a fixed unit normal selected at the left sample, the initially separated projections stay separated by at least `gap_left - L * (time - left)`. An independently selected right-sample axis similarly guarantees `gap_right - L * (right - time)`. If the two positive guaranteed neighborhoods cover the entire interval, and a positive numerical tolerance is retained, the source labels that **one interval** *nominally separated under the original conditional free-flight model*.

Otherwise it remains **uncovered/unknown**, even if the samples at both ends are nonoverlapping. This is strictly a sufficient nonintersection test, not a necessary collision test. A failed bound never implies that a contact happened.

## Independent witness and sub-grid counterexample

The source constructs world-space SAT margins directly. A different independent verifier reconstructs the square's four corners in a distinct ordering and derives separating normals from polygon edges and the finite segment. It independently checks **every one** of the 2×2,048 grid intervals in each of the six admitted cases, including original Trial25 ancestry, source segments, speed bound, reported coverage/unknown counts, minimum covered surplus, and tested physical-claim limits.

For additional falsification, a synthetic square moving extremely quickly across a fixed vertical finite segment intersects it **between two separated adjacent sample poses**. That test must remain uncovered by the envelope. The independent verifier also checks a midway pose for every claimed covered interval, but that mid-interval sample is an extra counterexample check, not the proof.

The result uses ordinary floating-point evaluations plus a positive numerical margin, **not outward-rounded interval arithmetic** or mathematically certified real-number exactness. Its nominal separation guarantee is conditional on the stated rigid-body velocity bound and original free-flight assumptions. It does not authenticate real materials or a fourth collision. Following any modeled earlier contact, continued original ballistic movement remains explicitly **counterfactual**, even if later grid values are numerically produced.
