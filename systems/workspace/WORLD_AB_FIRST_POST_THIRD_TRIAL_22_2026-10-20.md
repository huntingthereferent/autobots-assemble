# WORLD Trial 22 — next possible movement after conditional third impulse

This is another **bounded experiment in the existing WORLD**, not a new general physics engine, a material-law catalogue, or a physical device.

## Original movement and question

The original Trial 20 constructed six conditional virtual receivers from the rotating square's source-derived movement. The original Trial 21 tested six conditional third impulses and geometric separation within the next `0.008` virtual-time units. It did not test what else could be encountered after that interval.

Trial 22 asks one new question: **within an additional bounded `0.25` virtual-time projection from the third impulse, where would the rotating square's four vertices first cross one of the existing original source/receiver supporting lines or four world boundaries?**

No target fourth collision is provided in advance. The square's initial center, angle, outgoing velocity, and angular velocity come from the original Trial 21 conditional result. The original 24×6 world, original finite source face, and movement-derived finite receiver are retained unchanged.

Each corner's oriented signed plane distance has the form `c + v*t + A*cos(omega*t) + B*sin(omega*t)`. Source Trial 22 partitions this function at its analytic stationary times and bisects the first modeled inward crossing on each corner/face pair. The source retains **all** crossing candidates, not just a convenient one, and keeps co-earliest alternatives when two features coincide.

A line crossing outside a **finite** face's endpoints is not promoted to a physical or finite-segment collision. Ambiguous endpoint hits and simultaneous candidates remain unresolved. A window without source crossings is labeled only as a *bounded no-crossing result under the projected model*.

## Independent observation and falsification

The independent Trial 22 verifier first reruns the original Trial 21 independent impulse and local-bound checks. It then recomputes all four rotating vertex trajectories and all six line geometries using a **numerical derivative sign-sweep and bisection**, independently of the generator's analytic stationary-time expressions. It challenges crossing chronology, full candidate records, transformed vertices, contact-surface identity, inward normal rate, finite-segment tangent coordinate, source ancestry, fabricated later impulses, and hardware/formal-certification claims.

A numerical witness with ordinary Python floats does not guarantee mathematically certified roots. It is neither a continuous measured sensor record nor a proof that the original conditional third impulse actually happened. No fourth impulse, extra face, higher-level physical law, or later post-collision trajectory is constructed.
