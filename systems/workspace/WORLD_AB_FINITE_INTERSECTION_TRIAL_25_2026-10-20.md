# WORLD Trial 25 — complete finite segment versus moving square

## A different test from Trials 22–24

Trials 22 and 23 examined original square *vertices* crossing the supporting lines of the source face, motion-derived receiver and world perimeter. Trial 24 checked the two *endpoints* of each finite source face against the four moving square edges. Its source evidence included 34 crossings of edge-line extensions but no finite endpoint–edge interior event in the eight controlled cases.

These results do not directly establish the overall finite **intersection state** of the complete source segment and rotating square. Trial25 therefore asks whether either already existing finite segment actually intersects the original moving square at any sampled time within the same source-conditioned virtual window.

The original model remains the 24×6 WORLD, original finite face, existing motion-derived receiver, four-vertex rigid square, and original WORLD21 **unselected** third impulse. The same two no-impulse cases remain closed. No hardware or new object is constructed.

## Bounded detection, independent relationship

For each of six source-conditioned cases, sample both complete finite source segments against the original rotating square at 2,049 times from `t=0.008` through `t=0.25` relative to the hypothesized third impulse.

The source tests overlap by rotating each finite source segment into the moving square's local coordinates and applying a **finite segment-to-square Liang–Barsky clipping** calculation. It retains sampled overlap counts, full grid-detected overlap windows, the first grid onset, and a bisected transition bracket when the previous sampled state is nonintersecting.

The independent witness uses a *different geometric calculation*: reconstruct the square as four points in world coordinates, then use the **separating-axis theorem** on the finite segment and square, with the square's two local normals and the finite segment's normal. For each actual source angle and both faces it independently checks the entire declared grid, first onset, finite overlap windows, bracketed candidate, original source ancestry and source status. A synthetic translating-square case demonstrates an independently expected first finite overlap.

The source also records a **same-face temporal comparison** for the earliest detected overlap, contrasting its two grid-bracketing times only with original Trial23 finite-face vertex roots on that exact face. It independently labels agreement within grid resolution, prior-vertex-earlier, prior-vertex-later, or no prior eligible vertex. This is a comparison of numerical source candidates, not a fourth-contact selection.

These are finite **numerical detection** and candidate brackets. They do not certify every possible short between-sample collision or tangency, formally prove the first possible contact time, or select any impulse response. Any movement after a detected candidate is still a counterfactual ballistic projection, not a witnessed material trajectory. Sources 22–24 remain intact as separate feature evidence.
