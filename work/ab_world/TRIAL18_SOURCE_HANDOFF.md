# WORLD18 — proposed second impulse at the finite first-return point

The original WORLD Trial17 source identified the first renewed finite-face
contact candidate for a freely rotating square, after its original Trial04
oblique impact. WORLD18 makes one additional *conditional model* test at that
same source-owned corner and time: apply the **single frictionless normal,
perfectly elastic rotating-square impulse assumption**, then examine a
very short projected postcontact interval.

Original source and numerical oracle, pinned to
`8f9600adcee76f830ac7eb769603e615f14179c9`, report six conditional
second-impulse computations and two source cases without a first recontact
candidate. Each proposed impulse is independently checked for source
corner identity, momentum/torque, normal restitution and energy balance.
Six 0.016-second windows contain 33 samples each, giving **198
hypothetical postimpact samples**. At these exact source settings no
sampled overlap was detected, but this is **not continuous clearance or
admitted postcontact physics**.

The neutral A/B carrier creates eight independently bounded source
records, with original modeled first impacts and source-owned Trial17 first
return information. Only cases with an admitted first return receive an
explicitly labeled conditional impulse object, introduced *after* the
source reported candidate impulse. Finite proposed position/orientation
samples are recorded as hypothetical model revisions and attributed
readings, never as observed material trajectories. Samples after any
source-detected overlap remain counterfactual.

Two no-return controls carry neither a second impulse nor projected
postreturn motion. No original 04B/Trial17/Trial18 equations are copied or
changed. Source-native verification remains responsible for the
mathematical candidate; A/B independently verifies the bounded record
and source-attached event chronology. The ledgers are linked by a manifest,
not pretended to share a global mutable object history.

**Unresolved:** No uniquely selected material impulse, no real sensor
measurements, no continuous separating-body proof, no further impacts,
and no movement-derived receiver. In particular, six passing *sample
windows* cannot establish a general rotating contact solver, Earth-material
behavior, a motor or NIH prototype, or a new universal A/B law.

Original WORLD sources, W13, ARC, NIH, renderer, World48, general
construction mechanics and All Rights Reserved licensing remain untouched.
