# WORLD27 — sampled overlap is not unproved clear-endpoint space

The original WORLD25 checks two unchanged finite source segments against
the whole moving square on a fixed time grid. WORLD26 separately applies a
sufficient nominal separating-axis/speed-envelope bound to the **intervals
between** samples. A failed sufficient bound says *uncovered*, not *collision*.

Pinned original WORLD27, at
`d1268d30c58825500354137ebdd3b140a131a84e`, independently reconstructs
each of the 2,048 intervals per original finite face and distinguishes:

- **SAMPLED OVERLAP ADJACENT:** At least one interval endpoint has an
  existing source25 finite shape intersection on the virtual sample grid
- **CLEAR-ENDPOINT UNKNOWN:** Both endpoint samples separate, but WORLD26's
  nominal speed/margin bound cannot support the full intervening time
- **NOMINALLY SEPARATED:** WORLD26's sufficient margin bound succeeds for
  the full interval, conditional on its original model

In the frozen original eight-case study, six cases admit a hypothetical,
unselected third impulse and two do not admit that motion at all. Across
the six modeled cases there are exactly **24,576 original face intervals**:

| Source-owned classification | Intervals |
|---|---:|
| Sampled finite overlap adjacent | 2,290 |
| Clear endpoint unknown | 0 |
| Nominally supported separation | 22,286 |

Thus all 2,290 intervals WORLD26 called *uncovered* are explained by
an **observed overlap at a sampled virtual endpoint**, not by presently
unknown clear-endpoint motion. These are not 2,290 individual collisions;
neighboring intervals share sample endpoints. And none establishes an
instrument-observed material impact, an exact first-contact time or a
fourth impulse.
